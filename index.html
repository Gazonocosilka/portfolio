Case Study · 2025

V&V Boutique

Role
UX Research · UX Design
Timeline
8 weeks
Scope
E-commerce Redesign
Tools
Figma · FigJam
01 — Overview

A premium fashion boutique with a split identity.

V&V Boutique is a Ukrainian-founded, London-based premium fashion brand. Their website tells a beautiful brand story — but the shopping experience felt like a different website entirely.

"The whole journey is itself the design problem — not just any single page."

02 — Problem

Discovery vs. decision fatigue.

Entry
Hero — brand story
Browse
Shop grid
⚠ Tension
Product page — gaps
Escape
Lookbook
⚠ Late signal
Checkout info

Users reached the product page with intent to purchase — then hesitated. Three questions went unanswered: Will it fit? What does it look like styled? What are the returns?

03 — Research

Three friction points. One root cause.

01
Product pages lacked size guides and styling context at the point of decision
02
Trust content (size chart, FAQ, returns) was siloed from discovery moments
03
Delivery and returns info appeared at checkout — too late to reduce hesitation
04 — Strategy

Bridge the gap. Don't redesign from scratch.

The goal was not to rebuild the site. The strategy was to integrate trust signals into the product experience, bringing the warmth of the brand narrative into the moment of decision.

1. Contextual sizing — Embed size guide directly on product pages


2. Lookbook integration — Pull editorial styling into product pages


3. Early trust signals — Surface delivery and returns on product pages

05 — Visual System

Preserving the brand's emotional language.

Colour palette

Playfair Display — Editorial voice

Inter — Functional clarity

06 — Outcome

What changes when friction becomes confidence.

User clarity
Users can answer their core questions without leaving the product page
Trust signals
Returns and delivery info surfaced early — not hidden at checkout
Brand coherence
Editorial and transactional experiences now feel like one unified voice
Journey loop
Lookbook → Product → Trust → Purchase creates a full, closed loop
07 — Reflection

What I learned.

The most important insight from this project wasn't about UI at all — it was about information architecture as an emotional experience. Where trust signals appear in a journey matters as much as whether they appear at all.

"Design is not just what something looks like. It's whether it works for the person standing in the moment of decision."
